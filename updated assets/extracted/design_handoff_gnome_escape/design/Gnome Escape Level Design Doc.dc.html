Gnome Escape Levels 2–6 · Design Doc
Single-screen co-op puzzle-platformer · 960×560 canvas · internal draft

Level design · Draft 1

Five rooms after the ruins

Levels 2 through 6 for Gnome Escape, each a single enclosed screen in the same 960×560 frame as Level 1. Every room adds two mechanics to the vocabulary and requires both gnomes to leave together. Blueprints below are schematic: they fix the beats, the ordering and the failure states, not the final tile art.

Stone Gnome · WASD

Heavy. Pushes blocks, breaks cracked walls, holds pressure plates, ignores spikes. Sinks in water, shoved by gusts, falls through brittle floors.

Wind Gnome · Arrows

Light. Double-jumps, glides, rides updrafts, crosses water and brittle floors. Too light to push blocks or hold plates; carried off course by gusts.

Introduction order

One new idea per gnome per room, then a room that asks for all of it. Nothing appears in a puzzle before it has appeared as a safe, obvious demonstration one room earlier.

1 · Overgrown Ruins Water, lever bridge, timed platform, gust, plate + block — Low — parallel paths

Note on Level 1

Level 1 as built teaches five mechanics in one screen. Recommend cutting the timed platform and the plate-and-block sequence out of it and letting the room be water, the lever bridge and the gust only — the two cut pieces are re-taught with room to breathe in 4 and 2. That also gives the opening room a clean three-beat rhythm instead of five.

Level 2 · The Cracked Vault

New: Breakable walls New: Hold-switch Returns: Plate + block

A sealed antechamber. The room's job is to establish that Stone can open geometry and that a switch can need a body left behind on it. It ends by teaching the latch — a block on a plate holds open what a gnome cannot stay to hold.

Walkthrough

  1. 1 — Stone walks into the cracked wall and breaks it. Wind cannot; the wall is the room's first statement of who opens geometry.
  2. 2 — Both cross into the middle chamber. Wind stands on the hold-switch. The gate rises only while weight is on it, and Wind is the one who can be spared.
  3. 3 — Stone passes through the open gate into the third chamber.
  4. 4 — Stone pushes the block onto the pressure plate. The plate latches the gate open permanently. Wind steps off the switch, crosses, and both take the exit.

Designer notes

Why it holds. The naive attempt is Stone on the switch and Wind through the gate — which fails, because Wind cannot move the block. The failure is instant, legible and costs four seconds, so players reason their way to the right assignment rather than guessing.

Failure states. None fatal. No water, no void, no gust. The room is a safe teaching space; the only cost is walking back.

Build note. The hold-switch is a new state on the existing lever: lever.held recomputed each frame from overlap rather than latched once. Keep the latched variant for Level 1's bridge.

Level 3 · Updraft Shaft

New: Updraft column New: Glide to ledge Returns: Water, lever

The first room where Wind goes somewhere Stone cannot follow, and comes back with a path. Water is the wall; the updraft is the way over it, but only for one gnome.

Walkthrough

  1. 1 — Wind jumps out over the pool and into the updraft column. The column carries it above the water; Stone entering the same column is shoved sideways into the pool, so the demonstration has to happen with Wind.
  2. 2 — Wind glides off the top of the column onto the high ledge and throws the lever.
  3. 3 — Three stepping stones drop into the pool. Stone crosses on them.
  4. 4 — Both climb the raised east ledge — Stone by jump, Wind by glide — and reach the exit.

Designer notes

Tuning. Column height must put Wind's apex a little above the ledge, not level with it, so the landing is forgiving. With the current jump and glide values that is roughly 130–140 px of lift over a 50 px-wide column.

Failure states. Stone drowning is the one death here, and it should be easy to trigger early — the pool is wide and right next to the start. Losing Stone to the water in the first ten seconds is the fastest way to teach the rule.

Softening. If playtests stall, widen the east ledge's approach rather than lowering the column; players read a missed jump as their mistake and a missed updraft as the game's.

Level 4 · Brittle Causeway

New: Cracked floors Returns: Timed platform, lever

The gnomes cross the same chasm by two different routes at the same time. Wind takes the high causeway that would collapse under Stone; Stone takes the platform Wind's lever set moving.

Walkthrough

  1. 1 — Wind double-jumps onto the brittle causeway and runs it. The slabs hold; a faint crack sound plays under each step so the material still reads as unsafe.
  2. 2 — Wind reaches the east floor and throws the lever. The timed platform starts its cycle across the chasm.
  3. 3 — Stone boards the platform at the west edge and rides across, stepping off before it reverses.
  4. 4 — Both reach the exit. Stone never touches the causeway; if it does, the slab it lands on falls away and Stone follows it into the void.

Teaching the material. Put one brittle slab on the west floor, over a shallow one-tile pit, before the causeway. Stone breaks it, drops a body-height, climbs out unharmed. The lesson costs nothing there and everything on the causeway.

Timing. Platform travel should be slow enough that Stone can miss a boarding and simply wait for the next pass. Nothing in this room should demand a frame-accurate step; the pressure is in coordination, not reflex.

Respawn. Broken slabs restore on reset. Do not let a player permanently strand themselves by breaking the causeway from underneath — there is no path back up from the void, so the void must reset the level rather than trap.

Level 5 · Crosswind Gallery

New: Gust as traversal New: Block as windbreak Returns: Bridge lever, plate

The same gust is Wind's only way across and the reason Stone cannot cross. Blocking a fan is a one-way decision, so this is the first room where doing the right things in the wrong order forces a reset.

Walkthrough

  1. 1 — Wind goes first. It jumps into gust 1 and is carried over the chasm to the east floor. This must happen before anything else is touched.
  2. 2 — Stone throws the lever, raising the bridge over the chasm — but gust 1 blows Stone off it. Stone pushes the west block into the fan's mouth, killing the airflow.
  3. 3 — With the gust dead, Stone walks the bridge across.
  4. 4 — Wind cannot stand on the exit ledge while gust 2 blows across it. Stone pushes the east block onto the plate, gust 2 stops, and both take the exit.

The trap, on purpose. A player who plugs gust 1 before Wind has crossed strands Wind on the west side with no route. That is the intended lesson of the room, and it needs to be recoverable in one keystroke — R resets instantly, and the reset message should name the cause: “Wind Gnome has no way across.”

Read the wind. Both gusts need constant particle flow, even while inert, so a plugged fan is visibly plugged rather than ambiguously broken. Gust 2 keeps a stub of particles behind the block.

Cut if it drags. If the room tests long, drop the second block and plate and let gust 2 pulse on Level 1's on/off cycle instead — Wind then simply times its arrival. Keeps the beat, halves the solve.

Level 6 · The Two Doors

Finale Returns: Everything

A pillar splits the room, and each gnome starts on the wrong side of it. Two exits, one per gnome, and they only count when both are occupied at once — so the room ends with the gnomes further apart than they have ever been.

Walkthrough

  1. 1 — Stone, on the east side, breaks the cracked wall. Behind it is the mouth of the tunnel that runs under the pillar.
  2. 2 — Stone takes the tunnel west and climbs the shaft onto the west floor, on the far side of the water.
  3. 3 — Wind climbs the west ledges over the water and glides through the pillar window to the east side, then crosses the brittle causeway.
  4. 4 — Stone stands in the west door, Wind stands in the east door. Both must be occupied on the same frame; either gnome leaving its door cancels the sequence.

The swap. Nothing new is taught. The difficulty comes entirely from the starting positions: each gnome begins in the half designed against it, so the first thirty seconds are spent reading the room rather than moving through it.

Two doors, one condition. The paired-exit check replaces the shared exit pad from Levels 1–5. Both doors light and hum while occupied so a waiting player can see their partner is not yet in place.

Sightlines. The pillar must not hide either gnome. Keep it narrow, and if the window and the tunnel both sit behind it, thin it to a colonnade rather than a solid slab — players need to watch each other work.

Open question. The tunnel's west shaft is unresolved: a climbable shaft, a one-way updraft, or a second cracked wall. A shaft is simplest but gives Stone a vertical move it has nowhere else.

Open questions

  • Does Level 1 get trimmed as recommended, or do 2–6 assume it stays as built?
  • Shared exit pad or paired doors as the standard? Level 6 needs paired doors; if that becomes the rule, Levels 2–5 should be rebuilt around it for consistency.
  • Is a per-room reset acceptable as the punishment for wrong ordering, or does Level 5 need an undo — a block a gnome can pull as well as push?
  • Tile size. Every blueprint here is drawn on a 32 px grid; the existing level uses free-floating rectangles. Committing to tiles now makes the art pipeline much simpler later.
